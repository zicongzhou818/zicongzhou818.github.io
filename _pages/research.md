My name is Zicong Zhou, a computational mathematician who has been developing the Deformation/Tensor-Based Morphometry (DBM/TBM) methods for neuroscience research. My primary research lies at the intersection of computational mathematics, numerical analysis, and neuroimage processing + analysis, for both relevant mathematical theories and practical applications to real-world neuroimage data, such as Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) modalities T1, T2, diffusion-MRI, and functional-MRI. 

I specialize in the domain of grid generation, a subfield of computational diffeomorphisms, which I used for the development of rigorous mathematical frameworks for 3D non-rigid medical image registration. I developed the VPreg framework to map individual brain variabilities accurately from one to another. Currently, I am building a novel Brain Atlas Construction method based on VPreg, which is named VPatlas.

Looking forward, my research is structured into short-term and long-term agendas. In the near future, my goals focus on extending the VPreg framework to handle multi-modal anatomical data, while leveraging GPU-accelerated solvers and numerical optimization techniques to shorten the time-to-solution. Meaning, to make the computational tools faster while keeping accuracy. In the long term, I aim to pioneer advanced multi-modal investigations that fuse high-dimensional neuroimaging phenotypes with complex genomic data, applying high-dimensional probability, statistical learning theory, and stochastic optimization to model complex biological variability. This has the potential to link the image representation and the genomic expression.
